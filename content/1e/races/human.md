# Human

## Overview

Humans are the standard race for First Edition play.

They have no unusual racial powers, but they have the cleanest rules and the widest long-term class freedom.

## Ability Considerations

Humans do not have special racial ability adjustments in this section.

If your ability scores qualify for a class, Human usually keeps the path simple.

There are no race-based Human minimums or maximums listed in the race section.

## Allowed Class Considerations

Allowed single classes:

- Assassin
- Cleric
- Druid
- Fighter
- Illusionist
- Magic User
- Paladin
- Ranger
- Thief

Multi-class availability:
None from race.

Unavailable classes:
None from race.

Special notes:
Humans are the safest choice if you want to choose class without race restrictions.

## Languages

Starting Languages:

- Common
- Alignment language

Additional Languages:
Use the additional language allowance from Intelligence when the Languages step tells you to choose them.

What Player Records:
Write `Common, alignment`.

## Movement

Movement:
12"

Write:
120 ft

Meaning:
Used later during exploration and combat. You move at the standard adventuring pace for a man-sized character.

## Vision

Humans do not receive infravision from race.

Light, torches, lanterns, and marching order matter more for Human characters.

## Special Abilities

Humans have no special racial ability package.

Their strength is freedom: fewer restrictions, fewer exceptions, and strong long-term class potential.

## Restrictions

Humans have fewer race-based restrictions than demi-humans.

Assassin and druid advancement are special cases handled by class rules.

## Advancement Notes

Race affects advancement:
No special race limit for most classes.

Humans are the best choice for campaigns expected to reach high levels.

Assassin and Druid have class-specific limits handled on the Class page.

## Play Experience

Human is direct and flexible.

You spend less time managing race exceptions and more time learning class, equipment, and table procedure.

## Who Should Play This

Choose Human if you want:

- A straightforward first character
- Widest class freedom
- Long-term growth
- A concept defined by class and choices more than ancestry

## Example Character Concepts

- Ambitious fighter seeking a lordship
- Village cleric drawn into danger
- Apprentice magic user with one dangerous spellbook
- Clever thief from a city alley

## Beginner Notes

Do not treat Human as boring.

In First Edition, fewer racial powers often means fewer racial limits.
