# Dwarf

## Overview

Dwarfs are durable underground adventurers.

They are strong choices for players who want toughness, dungeon awareness, and a practical front-line role.

## Ability Considerations

Dwarfs adjust toward endurance and away from easy social influence.

They gain Constitution and lose Charisma for most social purposes outside dwarf society.

Dwarf scores must fit these limits after racial adjustment:

- Strength: at least 8
- Constitution: at least 12
- Dexterity: no higher than 17
- Charisma: no higher than 16
- Intelligence and Wisdom: normal 3-18 range

Check your scores before choosing Dwarf, especially if you want a nimble or highly charismatic character.

## Allowed Class Considerations

Allowed single classes:

- Assassin
- Cleric
- Fighter
- Thief

Multi-class availability:

- Fighter/Thief

Unavailable classes:

- Druid
- Illusionist
- Magic User
- Paladin
- Ranger

Special notes:
Dwarfs are strongest as Fighters, Thieves, or tough hybrid dungeon characters.

## Languages

Starting Languages:

- Common
- Alignment language
- Dwarfish
- Gnomish
- Goblin
- Kobold
- Orcish

Additional Languages:
A dwarf may learn no more than two additional languages beyond the starting list, even with high Intelligence.

What Player Records:
Write `Common, alignment, dwarfish, gnomish, goblin, kobold, orcish`.

## Movement

Movement:
9"

Write:
90 ft

Meaning:
Used later during exploration and combat. You move slower than races with 12" movement.

## Vision

Dwarfs have infravision 60'.

Infravision lets you see heat sources in darkness. It does not replace normal light for reading, mapping, or allies without dark vision.

## Special Abilities

Dwarfs resist magic and poison well, with that resilience tied to Constitution.

They are especially alert to stonework and underground construction.

Write these as race notes:

- Bonus on saves against magic and poison, based on Constitution.
- Better attacks against goblins, half-orcs, hobgoblins, and orcs.
- Harder for giants, ogres, ogre mages, titans, and trolls to hit.
- Can check stonework for slopes, new construction, shifting stone, stone traps, and depth underground.

When to use detection:
Tell the referee you are deliberately checking nearby stonework or underground features.

## Restrictions

Dwarfs have narrower class choices than Humans.

Their race can limit Dexterity and Charisma, and their movement is slower.

Multi-class Dwarfs use the stricter class requirement when class abilities conflict.

## Advancement Notes

Race affects advancement:
Yes.

Level limits:

- Assassin: up to level 9
- Cleric: up to level 8
- Fighter: up to level 9 with Strength 18, level 8 with Strength 17, or level 7 with Strength 16 or lower
- Thief: unlimited

Dwarfs cannot be Druids, Illusionists, Magic Users, Paladins, or Rangers.

## Play Experience

Dwarf feels sturdy and dependable.

You are good at surviving dungeon pressure, noticing underground details, and holding the line.

## Who Should Play This

Choose Dwarf if you want:

- A tough dungeon explorer
- Strong defenses against poison and magic
- A front-line or armored character
- Clear exploration utility

## Example Character Concepts

- Veteran tunnel fighter
- Stone-wise cleric of an ancestral shrine
- Treasure-seeking Fighter/Thief
- Grim assassin from a clan feud

## Beginner Notes

Do not choose Dwarf only for toughness.

Make sure the slower movement and narrower class list fit the character you want.
