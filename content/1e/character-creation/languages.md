# Languages

Languages tell the table who your character can bargain with, threaten, deceive, overhear, read, and understand.

They also say something about upbringing. A character who knows goblin learned it somewhere. A character who refuses to speak orcish may have a story behind that silence.

## Starting Languages

Characters begin knowing:

- Alignment language
- Common
- Racial languages where applicable
- Intelligence-based additional languages

Race can change the list. Use the Race page for your chosen ancestry, then add any extra languages allowed by Intelligence and race limits.

## Alignment Language

OSRIC characters normally know the language of their alignment.

This is not casual street speech. It is a strange shared language of signs, sayings, ritual phrases, old passwords, and supernatural sympathy. Use it carefully; speaking it openly may reveal more than intended.

## Common

Common is the trade and adventuring language most player characters can use to talk across ordinary human realms.

It is good enough for hiring porters, buying supplies, warning a guard, or making a desperate bargain in a border town. It does not replace older racial, monster, temple, guild, or magical languages.

## Racial Languages

Your race page lists the languages your character begins with.

Examples:

- Humans usually begin with Common and alignment language.
- Dwarves begin with Common, alignment, dwarfish, gnomish, goblin, kobold, and orcish.
- Elves begin with Common, elven, gnoll, gnomish, goblin, halfling, hobgoblin, and orcish.
- Half-orcs begin with Common and orcish.

Some races have limits on extra languages even with high Intelligence. Use the race entry when it is stricter than the general rule.

## Intelligence-Based Additions

Intelligence can grant additional languages. These are chosen during character creation, subject to referee approval and racial limits.

| Intelligence | Additional Languages |
| ---: | ---: |
| 3-7 | 0 |
| 8-10 | 1 |
| 11-12 | 2 |
| 13-14 | 3 |
| 15-16 | 4 |
| 17 | 5 |
| 18 | 7 |

Choose languages that fit the campaign. Good choices often include enemy languages, neighboring cultures, trade tongues, religious languages, or languages tied to the character's teacher.

## Examples

Human Fighter:
A human Fighter with Intelligence 12 records Common, alignment language, and two additional languages approved by the referee.

Dwarf Cleric:
A dwarf Cleric records the dwarf starting list from the Race page. If Intelligence would allow more, the dwarf still follows the dwarf limit of no more than two additional languages beyond the starting list.

Elf Magic User:
An elf Magic User records the elf starting list. If Intelligence is 16 or higher, the elf may add languages according to the elf racial limits.

## Write This Down

Where did you learn them?
Write a teacher, place, enemy, book, temple, guild, family, or military company.

Who taught you?
Name one person, culture, or institution.

What language do you avoid speaking?
Write one language that carries fear, shame, secrecy, danger, or old grief.
