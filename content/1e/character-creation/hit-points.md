# Hit Points

Hit points measure more than meat. They represent toughness, luck, endurance, grit, timing, divine favor, fighting sense, and the narrow margin by which an adventurer survives combat.

A character with hit points remaining can still act. A character reduced to the table's death threshold is in real danger, even if the last blow was described as exhaustion, blood loss, shock, or a weapon finally finding its mark.

## Rolling Hit Points

Each class has a hit die. At a new level, roll the class hit die, apply Constitution adjustment if any, and add the result to total hit points.

Some classes have special advancement details at higher levels, and Ranger begins sturdier than most warriors. When in doubt, use the class page for the exact advancement line.

## Drago Russo Rule

At First Level:

Roll normally.

Then set HP equal to maximum result.

Magic User:
Roll d4, then start with 4 hit points.

Fighter:
Roll d10, then start with 10 hit points.

Constitution still applies.

Future levels use normal rolling.

## Constitution

Apply Constitution hit point adjustment after the first-level maximum is set.

A Fighter with 10 first-level hit points and a +2 Constitution adjustment starts with 12 hit points.

A Magic User with 4 first-level hit points and no Constitution adjustment starts with 4 hit points.

## Example Progression

| Level | Fighter Example | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | First level d10 is maximized | 10 hp before Constitution |
| 2 | Roll d10 normally and add the result | 10 hp plus roll before Constitution |
| 3 | Roll d10 normally again | Previous total plus roll before Constitution |

## Write This Down

Hit Die:
Write your class hit die.

Starting Hit Points:
Write the first-level maximum, then apply Constitution.

Future Reminder:
After first level, roll normally when you gain a level.
