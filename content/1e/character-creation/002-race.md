## Introduction

Race is chosen after ability scores.

Your race shapes what classes you can choose, what languages you begin with, how fast you move, what you can see, and what special abilities you bring into the dungeon.

This page is not a teaser for another reference page. It gives you enough information to choose your race and continue to Class.

## How Race Works

First, check your ability scores.

Some races require certain minimum scores or place maximum limits on certain scores. If your scores do not qualify, that race is not available unless the referee says otherwise.

You may lower an ability score to qualify for a race. You may not raise a score for that purpose without referee permission.

Race can affect:

- Ability score adjustments
- Class eligibility
- Multi-class options
- Class advancement limits
- Languages
- Movement
- Infravision, which means seeing heat and shape in darkness
- Special senses or resistances
- Combat or exploration advantages

Humans are the baseline. Demi-human races often gain useful abilities, but they usually trade that power for class limits, advancement limits, or narrower options.

## Race Comparison

Human

## Human

### Overview

Humans are the standard race for First Edition play.

They have no unusual racial powers, but they have the cleanest rules and the widest long-term class freedom.

### Ability Considerations

Humans do not have special racial ability adjustments in this section.

If your ability scores qualify for a class, Human usually keeps the path simple.

There are no race-based Human minimums or maximums listed in the race section.

### Allowed Class Considerations

Allowed single classes:

- Assassin
- Cleric
- Druid
- Fighter
- Illusionist
- Magic User
- Paladin
- Ranger
- Thief

Multi-class availability:
None from race.

Unavailable classes:
None from race.

Special notes:
Humans are the safest choice if you want to choose class without race restrictions.

### Languages

Starting Languages:

- Common
- Alignment language

Additional Languages:
Use the additional language allowance from Intelligence when the Languages step tells you to choose them.

What Player Records:
Write `Common, alignment`.

### Movement

Movement:
12"

Write:
120 ft

Meaning:
Used later during exploration and combat. You move at the standard adventuring pace for a man-sized character.

### Vision

Humans do not receive infravision from race.

Light, torches, lanterns, and marching order matter more for Human characters.

### Special Abilities

Humans have no special racial ability package.

Their strength is freedom: fewer restrictions, fewer exceptions, and strong long-term class potential.

### Restrictions

Humans have fewer race-based restrictions than demi-humans.

Assassin and druid advancement are special cases handled by class rules.

### Advancement Notes

Race affects advancement:
No special race limit for most classes.

Humans are the best choice for campaigns expected to reach high levels.

Assassin and Druid have class-specific limits handled on the Class page.

### Play Experience

Human is direct and flexible.

You spend less time tracking race rules and more time learning class, equipment, and table procedure.

### Who Should Play This

Choose Human if you want:

- A straightforward first character
- Widest class freedom
- Long-term growth
- A concept defined by class and choices more than ancestry

### Example Character Concepts

- Ambitious fighter seeking a lordship
- Village cleric drawn into danger
- Apprentice magic user with one dangerous spellbook
- Clever thief from a city alley

### Beginner Notes

Do not treat Human as boring.

In First Edition, fewer racial powers often means fewer racial limits.

### Write This Down

Race:
Human

Languages:
Common, alignment.

Movement:
120 ft

Vision:
Normal vision

Special Notes:
No racial special abilities. Widest class freedom.

Class Restrictions:
None from race.

Advancement Notes:
No special race limit for most classes.

Future Reminder Notes:
Choose class normally; class-specific limits are checked on the Class page.

## Dwarf

### Overview

Dwarfs are durable underground adventurers.

They are strong choices for players who want toughness, dungeon awareness, and a practical front-line role.

### Ability Considerations

Dwarfs adjust toward endurance and away from easy social influence.

They gain Constitution and lose Charisma for most social purposes outside dwarf society.

Dwarf scores must fit these limits after racial adjustment:

- Strength: at least 8
- Constitution: at least 12
- Dexterity: no higher than 17
- Charisma: no higher than 16
- Intelligence and Wisdom: normal 3-18 range

Check your scores before choosing Dwarf, especially if you want a nimble or highly charismatic character.

### Allowed Class Considerations

Allowed single classes:

- Assassin
- Cleric
- Fighter
- Thief

Multi-class availability:

- Fighter/Thief

Unavailable classes:

- Druid
- Illusionist
- Magic User
- Paladin
- Ranger

Special notes:
Dwarfs are strongest as Fighters, Thieves, or tough hybrid dungeon characters.

### Languages

Starting Languages:

- Common
- Alignment language
- Dwarfish
- Gnomish
- Goblin
- Kobold
- Orcish

Additional Languages:
A dwarf may learn no more than two additional languages beyond the starting list, even with high Intelligence.

What Player Records:
Write `Common, alignment, dwarfish, gnomish, goblin, kobold, orcish`.

### Movement

Movement:
9"

Write:
90 ft

Meaning:
Used later during exploration and combat. You move slower than races with 12" movement.

### Vision

Dwarfs have infravision 60'.

Infravision lets you see heat sources in darkness. It does not replace normal light for reading, mapping, or allies without dark vision.

### Special Abilities

Dwarfs resist magic and poison well, with that resilience tied to Constitution.

They are especially alert to stonework and underground construction.

Write these as race notes:

- Bonus on saves against magic and poison, based on Constitution.
- Better attacks against goblins, half-orcs, hobgoblins, and orcs.
- Harder for giants, ogres, ogre mages, titans, and trolls to hit.
- Can check stonework for slopes, new construction, shifting stone, stone traps, and depth underground.

When to use detection:
Tell the referee you are deliberately checking nearby stonework or underground features.

### Restrictions

Dwarfs have narrower class choices than Humans.

Their race can limit Dexterity and Charisma, and their movement is slower.

Multi-class Dwarfs use the stricter class requirement when class abilities conflict.

### Advancement Notes

Race affects advancement:
Yes.

Level limits:

- Assassin: up to level 9
- Cleric: up to level 8
- Fighter: up to level 9 with Strength 18, level 8 with Strength 17, or level 7 with Strength 16 or lower
- Thief: unlimited

Dwarfs cannot be Druids, Illusionists, Magic Users, Paladins, or Rangers.

### Play Experience

Dwarf feels sturdy and dependable.

You are good at surviving dungeon pressure, noticing underground details, and holding the line.

### Who Should Play This

Choose Dwarf if you want:

- A tough dungeon explorer
- Strong defenses against poison and magic
- A front-line or armored character
- Clear exploration utility

### Example Character Concepts

- Veteran tunnel fighter
- Stone-wise cleric of an ancestral shrine
- Treasure-seeking Fighter/Thief
- Grim assassin from a clan feud

### Beginner Notes

Do not choose Dwarf only for toughness.

Make sure the slower movement and narrower class list fit the character you want.

### Write This Down

Race:
Dwarf

Languages:
Common, alignment, dwarfish, gnomish, goblin, kobold, orcish.

Movement:
90 ft

Vision:
Infravision 60'

Special Notes:
Magic and poison resistance by Constitution; stonework detection; bonuses against goblin/orc enemies; harder for giant-type foes to hit.

Class Restrictions:
Allowed Assassin, Cleric, Fighter, Thief, Fighter/Thief. Unavailable Druid, Illusionist, Magic User, Paladin, Ranger.

Advancement Notes:
Assassin 9, Cleric 8, Fighter 7-9 by Strength, Thief unlimited.

Future Reminder Notes:
Check Fighter level limit after recording Strength.

## Elf

### Overview

Elves are versatile adventurers with magic, scouting, weapon skill, and supernatural resilience.

They offer strong tools, but they also carry advancement limits and more rules to track.

### Ability Considerations

Elves lean toward Dexterity and away from Constitution.

They gain Dexterity and lose Constitution.

Elf scores must fit these limits after racial adjustment:

- Dexterity: at least 7 and no higher than 19
- Constitution: at least 8 and no higher than 17
- Intelligence: at least 8
- Strength, Wisdom, and Charisma: normal 3-18 range

Check this carefully if you want a durable front-line Elf.

### Allowed Class Considerations

Allowed single classes:

- Assassin
- Cleric
- Fighter
- Magic User
- Thief

Multi-class availability:

- Fighter/Magic User
- Fighter/Thief
- Magic User/Thief
- Fighter/Magic User/Thief

Unavailable classes:

- Druid
- Illusionist
- Paladin
- Ranger

Special notes:
Elves are especially notable for Fighter/Magic User and other fighter, magic, and thief combinations.

### Languages

Starting Languages:

- Common
- Elven
- Gnoll
- Gnomish
- Goblin
- Halfling
- Hobgoblin
- Orcish

Additional Languages:
An elf may learn additional languages only with Intelligence 16 or higher. Intelligence 16 allows one additional language, 17 allows two, and 18 allows three.

What Player Records:
Write `Common, elven, gnoll, gnomish, goblin, halfling, hobgoblin, orcish`.

### Movement

Movement:
12"

Write:
120 ft

Meaning:
Used later during exploration and combat. You move at the standard adventuring pace for a man-sized character.

### Vision

Elves have infravision 60'.

They also have a strong chance to notice or find secret and concealed doors.

Infravision lets you see heat sources in darkness. It does not replace normal light.

### Special Abilities

Elves resist sleep and charm effects.

They gain combat benefits with bows and with common elven swords.

They can surprise enemies more easily when scouting in the right conditions, especially when lightly armored and ahead of the party.

Write these as race notes:

- Strong resistance to sleep and charm.
- Bonus with pulled bows.
- Bonus with longsword and short sword.
- Can notice secret doors when passing nearby.
- Can find secret or concealed doors when actively searching.
- Better chance to surprise when scouting alone, far ahead, or with only elves and/or halflings.

### Restrictions

Elves have class advancement limits.

Thieving abilities still require armor allowed to thieves, even when the Elf is multi-classed.

Their lower Constitution ceiling can matter for survival-focused characters.

### Advancement Notes

Race affects advancement:
Yes.

Level limits:

- Assassin: up to level 10
- Cleric: up to level 7
- Fighter: up to level 7 with Strength 18, level 6 with Strength 17, or level 5 with Strength 16 or lower
- Magic User: up to level 11 with Intelligence 18 or higher, level 10 with Intelligence 17, or level 9 with Intelligence 16 or lower
- Thief: unlimited

Elves cannot be Druids, Illusionists, Paladins, or Rangers.

### Play Experience

Elf rewards players who like options.

You can fight, scout, cast, search, and solve problems, but you need to remember more special rules.

### Who Should Play This

Choose Elf if you want:

- Magic and weapons in one concept
- A capable scout
- Secret door awareness
- A character with flexible tactical options

### Example Character Concepts

- Fighter/Magic User with an old blade and a spellbook
- Silent archer scouting ahead of the party
- Magic User/Thief hunting hidden doors and old wards
- Exile from a strange woodland court

### Beginner Notes

Do not assume Elf is simply Human plus bonuses.

Elf is powerful, but the class and advancement limits are part of the choice.

### Write This Down

Race:
Elf

Languages:
Common, elven, gnoll, gnomish, goblin, halfling, hobgoblin, orcish.

Movement:
120 ft

Vision:
Infravision 60'

Special Notes:
Sleep/charm resistance; secret door detection; bow and sword bonuses; scouting surprise bonus in the right conditions.

Class Restrictions:
Allowed Assassin, Cleric, Fighter, Magic User, Thief, and listed fighter/magic/thief multi-classes. Unavailable Druid, Illusionist, Paladin, Ranger.

Advancement Notes:
Assassin 10, Cleric 7, Fighter 5-7 by Strength, Magic User 9-11 by Intelligence, Thief unlimited.

Future Reminder Notes:
Check Fighter or Magic User limit after choosing class.

## Gnome

### Overview

Gnomes are small underground specialists with strong resistance, useful dungeon senses, and access to illusion-focused play.

They are clever, technical, and a little more complex than Dwarfs.

### Ability Considerations

Gnomes require decent Constitution and Intelligence.

Gnome scores must fit these limits:

- Strength: at least 6
- Constitution: at least 8
- Intelligence: at least 7
- Dexterity, Wisdom, and Charisma: normal 3-18 range

### Allowed Class Considerations

Allowed single classes:

- Assassin
- Cleric
- Fighter
- Illusionist
- Thief

Multi-class availability:

- Fighter/Illusionist
- Fighter/Thief
- Illusionist/Thief

Unavailable classes:

- Druid
- Magic User
- Paladin
- Ranger

Special notes:
Gnomes are the natural choice if you want a small dungeon-wise Illusionist.

### Languages

Starting Languages:

- Common
- Dwarfish
- Gnomish
- Goblin
- Halfling
- Kobold

Additional Languages:
A gnome may learn no more than two additional languages beyond the starting list, even with high Intelligence.

What Player Records:
Write `Common, dwarfish, gnomish, goblin, halfling, kobold`. Also note communication with normal burrowing animals.

### Movement

Movement:
9"

Write:
90 ft

Meaning:
Used later during exploration and combat. You move slower than races with 12" movement.

### Vision

Gnomes have infravision 60'.

Infravision lets you see heat sources in darkness. It does not replace normal light.

### Special Abilities

Gnomes resist magic and poison well, with that resilience tied to Constitution.

They are alert to underground construction.

Write these as race notes:

- Bonus on saves against magic and poison, based on Constitution.
- Better attacks against kobolds and goblins.
- Harder for bugbears, giants, gnolls, ogres, ogre mages, titans, and trolls to hit.
- Can check stonework for slopes, unsafe walls, unsafe ceilings, unsafe floors, depth underground, and north underground.
- Can communicate with normal burrowing animals.

When to use detection:
Tell the referee you are deliberately checking nearby stonework or underground direction.

### Restrictions

Gnome multi-class characters are restricted to leather armor.

Class choices and advancement are narrower than Human.

### Advancement Notes

Race affects advancement:
Yes.

Level limits:

- Assassin: up to level 8
- Cleric: up to level 7
- Fighter: up to level 6 with Strength 18, or level 5 with Strength 17 or lower
- Illusionist: up to level 7 with very high Dexterity and Intelligence, level 6 if both are strong, or level 5 if either is lower
- Thief: unlimited

Gnomes cannot be Druids, Magic Users, Paladins, or Rangers.

### Play Experience

Gnome plays like a problem-solver.

You bring dungeon information, resistance, and unusual class options rather than raw simplicity.

### Who Should Play This

Choose Gnome if you want:

- A clever dungeon specialist
- Illusion magic
- Underground exploration tools
- A small character with strong defenses

### Example Character Concepts

- Illusionist who maps by echoes and shadows
- Fighter/Thief with a miner's eye for danger
- Cleric from a hidden burrow-shrine
- Assassin who uses tunnels and misdirection

### Beginner Notes

Gnome is not just a smaller Dwarf.

Choose it for illusion, scouting, and underground utility.

### Write This Down

Race:
Gnome

Languages:
Common, dwarfish, gnomish, goblin, halfling, kobold; communicate with normal burrowing animals.

Movement:
90 ft

Vision:
Infravision 60'

Special Notes:
Magic and poison resistance by Constitution; stonework/direction detection; bonuses against kobolds/goblins; harder for listed large foes to hit.

Class Restrictions:
Allowed Assassin, Cleric, Fighter, Illusionist, Thief, and listed fighter/illusionist/thief multi-classes. Unavailable Druid, Magic User, Paladin, Ranger.

Advancement Notes:
Assassin 8, Cleric 7, Fighter 5-6 by Strength, Illusionist 5-7 by Dexterity and Intelligence, Thief unlimited.

Future Reminder Notes:
If choosing Illusionist, check both Dexterity and Intelligence.

## Half-Elf

### Overview

Half-Elves are flexible bridge characters.

They keep some elven gifts while gaining a flexible set of class and multi-class choices.

### Ability Considerations

Half-Elves have forgiving ability requirements compared with stricter demi-human races.

Half-Elf scores must fit these limits:

- Dexterity: at least 6
- Constitution: at least 6
- Intelligence: at least 4
- Strength, Wisdom, and Charisma: normal 3-18 range

### Allowed Class Considerations

Allowed single classes:

- Assassin
- Cleric
- Fighter
- Magic User
- Ranger
- Thief

Multi-class availability:

- Cleric/Fighter
- Cleric/Ranger
- Cleric/Magic User
- Fighter/Magic User
- Fighter/Thief
- Magic User/Thief
- Cleric/Fighter/Magic User
- Fighter/Magic User/Thief

Unavailable classes:

- Druid
- Illusionist
- Paladin

Special notes:
Half-Elves are one of the best races for players who want a hybrid character.

### Languages

Starting Languages:

- Common
- Elven
- Gnoll
- Gnome
- Goblin
- Halfling
- Hobgoblin
- Orcish

Additional Languages:
Use the additional language allowance from Intelligence when the Languages step tells you to choose them.

What Player Records:
Write `Common, elven, gnoll, gnome, goblin, halfling, hobgoblin, orcish`.

### Movement

Movement:
12"

Write:
120 ft

Meaning:
Used later during exploration and combat. You move at the standard adventuring pace for a man-sized character.

### Vision

Half-Elves have infravision 60'.

They also have limited secret and concealed door detection.

Infravision lets you see heat sources in darkness. It does not replace normal light.

### Special Abilities

Half-Elves have partial resistance to sleep and charm effects.

They inherit some elven awareness without taking on every elven limit.

Write these as race notes:

- Partial resistance to sleep and charm.
- Can notice concealed doors when passing nearby.
- Can find secret or concealed doors when actively searching.

### Restrictions

Half-Elves still have advancement limits.

### Advancement Notes

Level limits:

- Magic User: up to level 8 with Intelligence 18 or higher, level 7 with Intelligence 17, or level 6 with Intelligence 16 or lower
- Ranger: up to level 8 with Strength 18, level 7 with Strength 17, or level 6 with Strength 16 or lower
- Thief: unlimited

Half-Elves cannot be Druids, Illusionists, or Paladins.

### Play Experience

Half-Elf is flexible but not effortless.

You get flexible build paths, but you still need to check how race affects your chosen class.

### Who Should Play This

Choose Half-Elf if you want:

- A hybrid class concept
- Flexible class access
- Some elven traits
- A character who can adapt to party needs

### Example Character Concepts

- Cleric/Fighter serving two traditions
- Fighter/Magic User trained between worlds
- Ranger with elven senses and human grit
- Magic User/Thief who solves problems quietly

### Beginner Notes

Do not choose Half-Elf because you cannot decide.

Choose it because flexibility is the character concept.

## Halfling

### Overview

Halflings are small, quiet, resilient adventurers.

They are excellent scouts and thieves, and they reward careful play more than brute force.

### Ability Considerations

Halflings lean toward Dexterity and away from Strength.

They lose Strength and gain Dexterity.

Halfling scores must fit these limits after racial adjustment:

- Strength: at least 6 and no higher than 17
- Dexterity: at least 8 and no higher than 19
- Constitution: at least 10
- Intelligence: at least 6
- Wisdom: no higher than 17
- Charisma: normal 3-18 range

### Allowed Class Considerations

Allowed single classes:

- Druid
- Fighter
- Thief

Multi-class availability:

- Fighter/Thief

Unavailable classes:

- Assassin
- Cleric
- Illusionist
- Magic User
- Paladin
- Ranger

Special notes:
Halflings are strongest when the player uses stealth, ranged attacks, and careful positioning.

### Languages

Starting Languages:

- Common
- Dwarfish
- Gnome
- Goblin
- Halfling
- Orcish

Additional Languages:
A halfling may learn one additional language for each point of Intelligence above 16, but may never learn more than two additional languages beyond the starting list.

What Player Records:
Write `Common, dwarfish, gnome, goblin, halfling, orcish`.

### Movement

Movement:
9"

Write:
90 ft

Meaning:
Used later during exploration and combat. You move slower than races with 12" movement.

### Vision

Halflings have infravision 60'.

Infravision lets you see heat sources in darkness. It does not replace normal light.

### Special Abilities

Halflings resist magic and poison well.

They are strong with bows and slings.

They can surprise enemies more easily when scouting in the right conditions, especially when lightly armored and away from the main group.

Write these as race notes:

- Bonus on saves against magic and poison, based on Constitution.
- Strong attack bonus with bow or sling.
- Better chance to surprise enemies when lightly armored and scouting alone, far ahead, or with only elves and/or halflings.

### Restrictions

Halflings have narrow class choices.

Their thieving abilities require armor allowed to thieves.

Their size and Strength limits make them less suited to heavy front-line play.

### Advancement Notes

Race affects advancement:
Yes.

Level limits:

- Druid: up to level 6
- Fighter: up to level 4
- Thief: unlimited

Halflings cannot be Assassins, Clerics, Illusionists, Magic Users, Paladins, or Rangers.

### Play Experience

Halfling play is about caution, quiet movement, and choosing the right moment.

You are not built to win every direct fight. You are built to avoid bad fights and make good ones easier.

### Who Should Play This

Choose Halfling if you want:

- A stealthy scout
- A small ranged combatant
- A resilient thief
- A careful dungeon survivor

### Example Character Concepts

- Sling-armed scout from a quiet village
- Thief who survives by patience and luck
- Fighter/Thief who opens the way for the party
- Druid with a strange bond to hidden places

### Beginner Notes

Do not play a Halfling like a small Human Fighter.

Use stealth, scouting, missile fire, and retreat routes.

### Write This Down

Race:
Halfling

Languages:
Common, dwarfish, gnome, goblin, halfling, orcish.

Movement:
90 ft

Vision:
Infravision 60'

Special Notes:
Magic and poison resistance by Constitution; bow/sling bonus; scouting surprise bonus in the right conditions.

Class Restrictions:
Allowed Druid, Fighter, Thief, Fighter/Thief. Unavailable Assassin, Cleric, Illusionist, Magic User, Paladin, Ranger.

Advancement Notes:
Druid 6, Fighter 4, Thief unlimited.

Future Reminder Notes:
Use stealth and ranged weapons; avoid assuming front-line Fighter play.

## Half-Orc

### Overview

Half-Orcs are physically hard adventurers suited to violence, survival, and dangerous work.

They have strong combat potential, but their social and class limits are real.

### Ability Considerations

Half-Orcs lean toward Strength and Constitution, but suffer heavily in Charisma.

They gain Strength and Constitution, and lose Charisma.

Half-Orc scores must fit these limits after racial adjustment:

- Strength: at least 6
- Constitution: at least 13 and no higher than 19
- Dexterity: no higher than 17
- Intelligence: no higher than 17
- Wisdom: no higher than 14
- Charisma: no higher than 12

### Allowed Class Considerations

Allowed single classes:

- Assassin
- Cleric
- Fighter
- Thief

Multi-class availability:

- Cleric/Fighter
- Cleric/Thief
- Cleric/Assassin
- Fighter/Thief
- Fighter/Assassin

Unavailable classes:

- Druid
- Illusionist
- Magic User
- Paladin
- Ranger

Special notes:
Half-Orcs cannot follow the more magical or idealized class paths.

### Languages

Starting Languages:

- Common
- Orcish

Additional Languages:
A half-orc may learn no more than two additional languages beyond the starting list, even with high Intelligence.

What Player Records:
Write `Common, orcish`.

### Movement

Movement:
12"

Write:
120 ft

Meaning:
Used later during exploration and combat. You move at the standard adventuring pace for a man-sized character.

### Vision

Half-Orcs have infravision 60'.

Infravision lets you see heat sources in darkness. It does not replace normal light.

### Special Abilities

Half-Orcs gain physical toughness and strength from race.

They pair well with class paths that reward violence, danger, stealth, or hard survival.

Write these as race notes:

- Strength and Constitution increase.
- Charisma decreases.
- Infravision 60'.
- Strong fit for Fighter, Assassin, Cleric, and Thief paths.

### Restrictions

Half-Orcs have significant class limits.

Their Charisma penalty can affect social situations, hirelings, and reactions.

For multi-class characters, armor follows the stricter class requirement, while weapons follow the more permissive requirement.

### Advancement Notes

Race affects advancement:
Yes.

Level limits:

- Assassin: up to level 15
- Cleric: up to level 4
- Fighter: up to level 10
- Thief: up to level 7 with Dexterity 17, or level 6 with Dexterity 16 or lower

Half-Orcs cannot be Druids, Illusionists, Magic Users, Paladins, or Rangers.

### Play Experience

Half-Orc play is direct, dangerous, and often socially tense.

You are good when the party needs force, intimidation, or ugly work done under pressure.

### Who Should Play This

Choose Half-Orc if you want:

- A hard combat character
- A Fighter, Assassin, Cleric, or Thief path
- A rough-edged dungeon survivor
- A character with real social friction

### Example Character Concepts

- Mercenary Fighter who survives by strength
- Assassin with a brutal reputation
- Cleric/Fighter from a harsh borderland cult
- Fighter/Thief who handles doors, threats, and dirty work

### Beginner Notes

Do not choose Half-Orc only for physical bonuses.

Be ready for limited class options and lower social ease.

### Write This Down

Race:
Half-Orc

Languages:
Common, orcish.

Movement:
120 ft

Vision:
Infravision 60'

Special Notes:
Strength and Constitution increase; Charisma decreases; limited class options; strong Fighter/Assassin/Cleric/Thief path.

Class Restrictions:
Allowed Assassin, Cleric, Fighter, Thief, and listed cleric/fighter/thief/assassin multi-classes. Unavailable Druid, Illusionist, Magic User, Paladin, Ranger.

Advancement Notes:
Assassin 15, Cleric 4, Fighter 10, Thief 6-7 by Dexterity.

Future Reminder Notes:

## Quick Decision Guide

Choose Human if you want the simplest path and strongest long-term freedom.

Choose Dwarf if you want toughness, dungeon senses, and a strong underground identity.

Choose Elf if you want weapons, magic, scouting, and secret-door awareness in one package.

Choose Gnome if you want underground expertise, illusion options, and clever utility.

Choose Half-Elf if you want flexible class choices and some elven abilities.

Choose Halfling if you want stealth, scouting, missile fire, and careful survival.

Choose Half-Orc if you want physical power, danger, and a harsher class path.

If you are stuck, choose the race that best supports the class you want next.
