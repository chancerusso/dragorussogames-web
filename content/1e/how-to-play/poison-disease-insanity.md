# Poison, Disease & Insanity

OSRIC treats poison, disease, and insanity as serious dangers. These rules are often triggered by monsters, traps, spells, or unusual hazards.

## Poison

When poison is ingested, inhaled, injected, or enters the bloodstream, the character normally makes a saving throw against poison.

The saving throw may be modified. OSRIC gives examples from +2 for a relatively weak toxin to -4 for a particularly lethal one.

If the save fails, the character dies. In practice, death is usually not instant, but the character is typically incapacitated immediately.

## Treating Poison

There is usually time for slow poison or neutralise poison if cast reasonably soon. OSRIC suggests about 15 minutes, depending on the venom or toxin.

If a character dies from poison, the poison must be removed from the bloodstream before raise dead or resurrection can succeed.

## Player Use Of Poison

OSRIC discourages easy player use of poison. Venom taken from a creature's glands usually loses effectiveness quickly and is fully denatured within a few hours.

Handling poison can risk accidental exposure. Some good-aligned classes, such as paladins, may not use poison and may object to companions using it.

## Disease

Diseases often come from monsters, where the monster text gives the chance and effect.

For plague, a character exposed to the disease saves against poison. On success, future saves against the same disease are at +4. On failure, after 2d8 days of incubation the character suffers -1d6 on all characteristics and die rolls for another 2d8 days.

If either duration die shows an 8, the character dies at the end of the disease. Otherwise recovery begins, reducing the penalty by 1 point per day until cured.

Wound infection uses the same effects, but onset is measured in hours rather than days.
