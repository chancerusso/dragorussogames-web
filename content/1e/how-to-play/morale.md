# Morale

Morale explains why monsters and followers do not always fight to the death. OSRIC morale applies to monsters, hirelings, henchmen, and men-at-arms, not player characters.

## When Morale Is Checked

The referee decides when morale checks occur. OSRIC suggests checking when creatures are clearly losing, taking unacceptable losses, seeing leaders fall, or facing a hopeless fight.

No more than two morale checks should be made per battle. If monsters pass a second morale check, they are assumed fanatical and fight to the death.

## Base Morale

Most monsters have base morale equal to 50% plus 5% per hit die. The referee adjusts this for cowardice, bravery, position, leadership, casualties, numbers, fear, and circumstances.

Player characters do not check morale. Hirelings, henchmen, and men-at-arms do.

## Morale Failure

| Failed By | Likely Result |
| ---: | --- |
| 25% or less | Fighting withdrawal |
| 26% to 50% | Turn and flee |
| 51% or more | Surrender |

The referee uses logic. A trapped creature may surrender instead of fleeing. A creature that knows surrender means execution will not surrender.

## Common Modifiers

| Situation | Modifier |
| --- | ---: |
| Per friend killed, surrendered, or fled | +5% |
| Own side took 25% casualties | +5% |
| Numerical inferiority | +10% |
| Own side took 50% casualties | +15% |
| Own side greatly outnumbered | +20% |
| Own leader out of combat | +25% |
| Per foe killed, surrendered, or fled | -5% |
| Own side inflicted 25% casualties | -5% |
| Numerical superiority | -10% |
| Own side inflicted 50% casualties | -15% |

In this table, positive modifiers make failure more likely and negative modifiers make failure less likely.

## Example At The Table

Six goblins lose their leader and half their number. The referee calls for morale. If they fail by a small margin, they back away while defending. If they fail badly, they run or throw down weapons.
