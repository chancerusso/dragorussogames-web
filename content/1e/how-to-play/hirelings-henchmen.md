# Hirelings & Henchmen

Adventurers often need help. OSRIC separates hirelings from henchmen.

Hirelings are usually zero-level workers, guards, torchbearers, craftspeople, or specialists. Henchmen are classed followers who can adventure and gain levels.

## General Rules

Hirelings and henchmen usually contribute only themselves. The player character who hires them must equip them.

If an already equipped NPC is hired, the value of that equipment should normally be paid as a stipend before service begins.

Most hirelings are assumed human unless the campaign says otherwise.

## Standard Hirelings

Standard hirelings are simple craftspeople and labourers. Short-term service is easy to find in large settlements and sometimes possible in villages.

Longer service is harder. If service longer than a few days is proposed, OSRIC suggests only 1 in 6 agrees, though extra pay can increase the chance as high as 1 in 2.

## Men-At-Arms

Men-at-arms require wages plus equipment costs. Their exact arms, armour, movement, and damage depend on type.

Common examples include bowmen, crossbowmen, footmen, horsemen, pikemen, sappers, slingers, marines, captains, lieutenants, and sergeants.

## Henchmen

Henchmen are more personal followers than ordinary hirelings. They are usually classed characters, begin at low level, and may gain levels.

Charisma affects how many henchmen a character can attract and how loyal they are.

## Loyalty And Morale

Hirelings and henchmen check morale. Their loyalty can be affected by pay, treatment, danger, the employer's Charisma, alignment, prior conduct, and whether they believe surrender or survival is possible.

## Example At The Table

The party hires two linkboys and a porter before entering a dungeon. They must pay wages, provide light and basic protection, and understand that these hirelings may check morale if the expedition turns deadly.
