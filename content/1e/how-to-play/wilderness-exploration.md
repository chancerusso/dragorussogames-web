# Wilderness Exploration

Wilderness exploration uses days rather than dungeon turns. Weather, navigation, encounters, movement, and camping are the central pressures.

## Daily Sequence

| Step | What Happens |
| --- | --- |
| Setup | The referee gives weather; the party chooses direction |
| Navigation | The referee secretly checks whether the party becomes lost |
| Wandering Monster Check | The referee checks for encounters |
| Move And Act | The party travels and performs stationary actions |
| Encounter | Any encounter is resolved |
| Camp | The referee describes campsite options; the party camps |
| Night Check | The referee checks again for wandering monsters |

## Getting Lost

The party does not become lost when following a road, river, accurate map, known terrain, or knowledgeable guide.

Otherwise, OSRIC suggests a 10% to 25% chance of getting lost across normal terrain with normal precautions. Bad terrain, darkness, fog, and poor conditions can make this worse.

If lost, the actual direction is determined randomly, usually within a 60 degree arc ahead, sometimes a wider arc on a bad result.

## Wilderness Encounters

OSRIC suggests two wandering monster checks during the day and a third at night, often with a 1 in 12 chance per day. Civilized patrols, dangerous wilderness, monster territory, and referee judgment adjust this.

Encounter mechanics are like dungeon encounters, but distance, mounts, missiles, and long-range spells become more important.

## Camping And Exhaustion

Spellcasters need camp and rest to regain spells. Mounted parties must rest animals. Armoured or physically weaker characters need sleep.

A forced march can continue through one night. A second night without rest imposes -2 or -10% on ability scores, attacks, damage, and saving throws. A third night increases the penalty to -5, and spellcasters begin forgetting memorized spells. No human or demi-human can go four nights without sleep.

Sensible parties set watches.
