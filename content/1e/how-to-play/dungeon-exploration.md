# Dungeon Exploration

Dungeon Exploration is the detailed version of the Exploration overview. OSRIC uses turns, meaningful choices, and resource pressure to keep dungeon play moving.

## Dungeon Turn Sequence

| Step | What Happens |
| --- | --- |
| Wandering Monster Check | Every third turn, usually 1 in 6 |
| Statement of Action | The party declares movement, listening, doors, searching, rest, spellcasting, or other work |
| Encounters | If contact occurs, determine surprise, distance, reactions, then negotiate, evade, or fight |
| Bookkeeping | Mark the turn and deduct resources such as light, spell durations, and hit points |

The referee may combine brief actions in one turn. Larger searches, door-breaking, or special tasks may take several turns.

## Movement And Marching Order

Cautious dungeon movement allows mapping. Passing through known areas can be up to 5 times faster, but no mapping is allowed.

Fleeing is 10 times normal per-turn movement, no mapping is possible, and a double rest period is required afterward.

The party should establish marching order. In a 10 ft corridor, up to three characters may walk abreast, reduced by large weapons or narrow spaces.

## Listening

Listening takes 1 round per attempt. Most characters have a 10% chance. Elves, gnomes, halflings, and half-orcs have a 15% chance. Thieves and assassins use class chances.

No more than three attempts may be made by the same character in the same situation before resting at least one turn.

## Doors

Forcing a stuck door takes 1 round per attempt. The standard chance is 2 in 6, adjusted by Strength.

Failed attempts are noisy, may increase wandering monster chances, and prevent surprise against creatures on the other side.

Chopping down a normal door takes at least 1 turn and may require several wandering monster checks.

## Mapping

The players' map represents an object in the game. The party needs light, mapping supplies, and slow movement.

Mapping exists to track explored areas, find the way out, return to a previous point, and detect loops, slopes, teleports, shifting rooms, or hidden spaces.

## Searching

Searching for secret doors takes 1 turn per 10 x 10 ft area. The standard chance is 1 in 6, or 2 in 6 for elves and half-elves.

Searching for traps usually takes 1-4 rounds and covers one object or a small area. Dwarfs, gnomes, thieves, and assassins have special chances.

The referee may reward carefully described searches, but slow detailed searches increase time pressure.

## Rest

Rest is normally required one turn in every six, one turn after combat, and two turns after evasion or pursuit.

Skipping rest causes fatigue at the referee's discretion: reduced movement, combat penalties, lower ability scores, or morale problems for NPCs.
